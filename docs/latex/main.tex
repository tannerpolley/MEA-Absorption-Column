    \begin{abstract}
        Post-combustion carbon dioxide (CO$_2$) capture with aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) is an established rate-based separation, but absorber comparisons often change thermodynamics, solver formulation, and evaluation scope simultaneously. This work uses one steady-state packed-column model to compare boundary-value problem (BVP) solvers and CO$_2$ fugacity closures while holding chemical equilibrium, reaction enhancement, transport correlations, hydraulics, and material and energy balances fixed. The routine thermodynamic campaign replaces the Henry-law CO$_2$ closure with a liquid-side ionic electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) fugacity calculation while retaining concentration-based chemical equilibrium with unit activity coefficients. For nine National Carbon Capture Center (NCCC) cases satisfying solver success, a boundary-residual norm no greater than 1.0, a predicted capture between 0 and 100\%, and a \qty{90}{s} wall-time limit, ePC-SAFT gave a capture mean absolute error of 3.32 percentage points, compared with 3.14 percentage points for Henry-law closure; the corresponding median runtimes were 7.70 and \qty{5.25}{s}. Collocation satisfied these criteria for the reported NCCC rows, whereas shooting had shorter recorded runtimes on smoother high-liquid-to-gas-ratio probes. A separate nine-species activity calculation, rebased to the concentration-based initial composition for each local chemistry call, returned solutions for all seven 2017 C rows under relaxed settings, with chemistry residuals below $10^{-8}$, capture mean absolute error of 6.595 percentage points, and mean runtime of \qty{171.102}{s}. This activity-rebased calculation tests numerical coupling and does not establish independently parameterized activity-basis reaction constants. The comparison reports attempted cases, residuals, runtimes, and capture errors under the stated equations, inputs, solver settings, and inclusion criteria.
    \end{abstract}
