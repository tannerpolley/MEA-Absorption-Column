    \begin{abstract}
        Post-combustion carbon dioxide (\COtwo) capture with aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) depends on coupled reaction equilibrium, thermodynamics and transport. This work evaluates a steady-state packed-column model with nine-species reactive electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT), conventional reaction enhancement and empirical column energy balances. Across seven National Carbon Capture Center (NCCC) one-bed cases, capture mean absolute error is \num{5.85} percentage points, with the largest signed errors of \num{+11.45} and \num{-11.93} points in Cases 6C and 7C. For the refined Case 3C reference, the selected model predicts \qty{91.55}{\percent} capture against \qty{89.50}{\percent} measured and a peak liquid temperature of \qty{347.86}{K}. Joint mesh/tolerance refinement changes capture by \num{0.00510} percentage points and peak temperature by \qty{0.04493}{K}. Five-percent thermodynamic-input perturbations change capture by up to \num{0.82} percentage points; ten-percent transport-input perturbations change it by up to \num{0.53} points. A historical nine-case comparison isolates the fugacity closure within a reduced six-species model: ePC-SAFT and Henry-law capture mean absolute errors are \num{3.32} and \num{3.14} percentage points, with median runtimes of \qty{7.70}{s} and \qty{5.25}{s}. Numerical-method comparisons are reported by configuration. The coupled result establishes a resolved reference case for sensitivity analysis; the local perturbations are not statistical uncertainty bounds.
    \end{abstract}
