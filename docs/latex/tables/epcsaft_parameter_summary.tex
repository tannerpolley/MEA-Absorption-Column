The segment mixing rules are $\sigma_{ij}=(\sigma_i+\sigma_j)/2$ and $\epsilon_{ij}=(1-k_{ij})\sqrt{\epsilon_i\epsilon_j}$.
The seven unit same-sign ion pair coefficients exclude their dispersion attraction.
All solvation factors are one except water, whose factor is 1.5.
The relative permittivity of MEA is 32 and the ionic-region value is 8.
Water uses the following temperature correlations, with $T$ numerically in kelvin:

 \sigma_{\HtwoO}(T)&=2.7927+10.11e^{-0.01775T}-1.417e^{-0.01146T}\quad(\unit{\angstrom}),\\
 \varepsilon_{r,\HtwoO}(T)&=78.41043478578715-0.36133766233691506(T-298.15)\notag\\
 &\quad+0.00076555618295(T-298.15)^2.
\end{align}
% B2: selected dielectric and Born equations
The model uses solvent-only mass-weighted relative permittivity,
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{r,\mathrm{bulk}}=
\frac{x_{\MEA}^{\mathrm{true}}M_{\MEA}\varepsilon_{r,\MEA}
+x_{\HtwoO}^{\mathrm{true}}M_{\HtwoO}\varepsilon_{r,\HtwoO}}
{x_{\MEA}^{\mathrm{true}}M_{\MEA}+x_{\HtwoO}^{\mathrm{true}}M_{\HtwoO}},\label{eq:solvent-permittivity}
\end{equation}
excluding ions and CO$_2$ from that solvent average.
The adopted Born expression includes solvation-shell and dielectric-saturation terms \cite{Figiel2025}.
With species solvation factors $s_i$ as specified above, the mixture factor and outer ion diameters are
\begin{align}
\overline s&=\sum_i x_i^{\mathrm{true}}s_i,&
d_i^{\mathrm{out}}&=d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}
\left[1+\frac{\overline s-1}{|z_i|}\right],\qquad z_i\ne0,\label{eq:born-shell}\\
D_i^{\mathrm{bulk}}&=\frac{1}{d_i^{\mathrm{out}}},&
D_i^{\mathrm{ion}}&=\frac{1}{d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}}-\frac{1}{d_i^{\mathrm{out}}}.\label{eq:born-diameters}
\end{align}
All nine positive solvation factors enter $\overline s$, so here $\overline s=1+0.5x_{\HtwoO}^{\mathrm{true}}$.
In \cref{eq:epcsaft-born}, the two regions use $D_i^{\mathrm{bulk}}$ with $\varepsilon_{r,\mathrm{bulk}}$ and $D_i^{\mathrm{ion}}$ with $\varepsilon_{r,\mathrm{ion}}=8$; diameters are converted to metres in that equation.
Both extension coefficients are one for these inputs, and no polar term is active.
