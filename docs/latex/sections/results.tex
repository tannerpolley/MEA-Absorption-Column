    \section{Results}
        Seven coupled nine-species NCCC calculations compare capture and temperature profiles with the 2017 C campaign. Case 3C provides the reference for numerical verification and local sensitivity. A separate historical six-species comparison isolates the fugacity closure across nine one-bed cases.

These checks establish numerical admissibility at the stated settings; the Case 3C refinement does not establish mesh convergence for all seven cases.     \subsection{ePC-SAFT Driving-Force Comparison}
        In the historical six-species comparison, the Henry-law baseline uses a liquid Henry-law \COtwo{} fugacity and an ideal vapor partial-pressure expression. The ePC-SAFT calculation replaces that closure with vapor- and liquid-side fugacity coefficients computed from pressure-specified phase states. The liquid phase uses the ePC-SAFT electrolyte configuration described in \cref{sec:model-framework}. The vapor-side calculation uses the same fugacity-coefficient framework with only neutral species present.

The left panel reports case-wise capture error, and the right panel reports median runtime.}
            \label{fig:nccc-one-bed-thermo-benchmark}

        \Cref{fig:nccc-one-bed-thermo-benchmark} reports the historical fixed-chemistry fugacity comparison. Across the included one-bed rows, ePC-SAFT gives a capture mean absolute error of \num{3.32} percentage points, compared with \num{3.14} percentage points for Henry-law closure. Its median runtime is \qty{7.70}{s}, compared with \qty{5.25}{s} for Henry-law closure. The \num{0.18}-percentage-point difference in mean absolute error does not show lower aggregate capture error for ePC-SAFT; the calculation instead quantifies the effect and runtime of replacing the Henry-law driving force with vapor- and liquid-side fugacity coefficients.

        The local sensitivity comparison uses the coupled nine-species Case 3C reference in \cref{fig:reactive-case3c-profiles} with the selected parameters (\cref{fig:reactive-parameter-sensitivity}). Its unperturbed capture is \qty{91.54839}{\percent} and maximum liquid temperature is \qty{347.86155}{K}. Multipliers of 0.95 and 1.05 on the MEA--water interaction change capture by \num{-0.82244} and \num{+0.75819} percentage points, respectively; the corresponding \COtwo--water changes are \num{+0.16272} and \num{-0.16576} points. Applying the same multipliers to the R4 equilibrium constant changes capture by \num{+0.42477} and \num{-0.41708} points; R5 gives \num{+0.58937} and \num{-0.59887} points. At equal relative perturbations, MEA--water gives the largest capture response, followed by R5, R4 and \COtwo--water. Each capture shift exceeds the \num{0.00510}-point reference refinement difference reported in \cref{tab:reactive-numerical-verification}. The perturbed capture range, \qtyrange{90.72595}{92.30657}{\percent}, remains above the observed \qty{89.50}{\percent}.

The thermodynamic and transport studies use different perturbation magnitudes, so their absolute changes do not establish a common parameter ranking.

    \subsection{Numerical Solver Behavior}
        The historical solver comparison answers a different question than the coupled reactive verification. It tests how shooting, collocation, and finite difference behave when they are applied to the same absorber equations under representative operating conditions. A smooth one-bed operating point provides a common comparison of the three tested configurations; physical admissibility is assessed separately from numerical termination. The NCCC 3C case has a sharper measured temperature feature. The \qty{89.40}{\percent} capture belongs to that historical method-comparison configuration, not the coupled nine-species campaign.

        The coupled nine-species Case 3C calculation provides a separate test of numerical resolution and computational expense (\cref{tab:reactive-numerical-verification}). Each calculation requires two mesh iterations and adds one node. Refinement reduces the maximum RMS differential residual from \num{0.125340} to \num{0.043138}; capture changes by \num{0.00510} percentage points and peak liquid temperature by \qty{0.04493}{K}. The maximum scaled boundary residual is zero in both calculations. On the 101 exported axial positions, net \COtwo{} and water flow vary by less than \qty{3e-14}{mol.s^{-1}}, all nine species concentrations are positive, and the axial net-enthalpy-flow range decreases from \qty{293.219}{W} to \qty{273.006}{W}. These observations support numerical resolution of this case; the refined capture of \qty{91.54839}{\percent}, compared with \qty{89.50}{\percent} measured, remains a separate physical comparison.

        Three sequential fresh-start Case 3C calculations using exact thermodynamic derivative reuse require BVP wall times of \qtyrange{34.41}{34.89}{s}, with a median of \qty{34.66}{s}. The fastest BVP run requires \qty{34.41}{s} wall time and \qty{34.23}{s} process CPU time; initialization, calculation and profile export together require \qty{51.45}{s}, with peak process resident memory of \num{203.51} MiB. Its mesh increases from 21 to 22 nodes in two iterations at collocation tolerance 0.5 and boundary tolerance 0.001. Capture is \qty{91.54329}{\percent}, with zero scaled boundary residual and maximum RMS residual \num{0.125340}. Across the exported numerical tables, differences from the coarse numerical reference, scaled by $\max(1,|\text{reference value}|)$, remain below \num{6.39e-10}. These measurements describe this discretization on a shared machine, not an accuracy-matched ranking of numerical methods.

        The original refinement calculations in \cref{tab:reactive-numerical-verification} require 335 and 499 liquid-equilibrium solves, including profile output. Their four Jacobian batches correspond to 84 and 164 local state Jacobians. These reuse previously evaluated thermodynamic states without additional liquid-equilibrium solves; derivative costs are incurred during the state evaluations. These counts belong to the refinement calculations, not the separate timing repeats.

    \subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
Two priorities for further analysis are joint thermodynamic and transport uncertainty and controlled variation of operating conditions.
The one-at-a-time interaction, equilibrium-constant and transport perturbations quantify local sensitivity at Case 3C; the chosen five- and ten-percent ranges are not fitted confidence intervals. Reaction-constant temperature dependence, gas-side transfer, effective interfacial area, kinetic and caloric inputs, correlated uncertainty and additional operating points would extend these comparisons.
Varying liquid-to-gas ratio, lean loading, and inlet temperature within the parameter domain would identify capture and temperature tradeoffs.
An energy or cost objective and hydraulic constraints are needed before interpreting an operating trend as an optimum.

A controlled comparison with eNRTL should first assemble complete, internally consistent Akula and/or Zhang parameterizations and reproduce their published VLE, speciation and caloric results \cite{Akula2023a,Zhang2011}. Those verified thermodynamic descriptions can then be compared with selected ePC-SAFT in the same conventional enhancement-factor column model, with transport and caloric choices explicitly matched. Combining published interaction coefficients with independently fitted reaction properties would instead define a new parameterization requiring its own verification. Controlled calibration subsets and held-out measurements are needed to test data efficiency and transferability before claiming easier regression for another solvent. Further column validation and controlled operating-condition studies would extend the present comparisons; resolving finite-rate liquid-film reaction and diffusion is a separate extension requiring independently evaluated kinetic and transport inputs.

        The absorber model is a simplified one-bed benchmark built from literature submodels for reaction enhancement, transport properties, hydraulics, and heat transfer. The coupled selected-parameter physical comparison contains Cases 1C--7C; the historical six-species set contains one-bed cases from the 2014 K and 2017 C campaigns. The numerical exclusions are summarized in \cref{tab:method-case-contrast}. The nine-case thermodynamic comparison holds the transport correlations fixed; the separate local sensitivity studies do not propagate joint parameter and correlation uncertainty.

        The NCCC cases span several flows, loadings, compositions, and temperatures, but those inputs co-vary as validation observations. They do not form a controlled operating sweep. The model also omits a plant-level energy or economic objective and fixes the one-bed pressure gradient at zero. No optimal operating condition is inferred from these rows. Plant optimization would require predeclared decision variables, hydraulic and process constraints, an energy or cost objective, and uncertainty analysis.

The governing balances can be applied to DEA, MDEA, AMP, PZ, or blended solvents only after replacing the solvent-specific submodels.
The extension begins with conserved components, a reaction network, and consistent activity and enthalpy reference states.
Pure-component, binary, association, ionic, and dielectric parameters must be obtained from applicable sources or fitted to independent thermodynamic measurements, including VLE, speciation, and caloric data where available.
The fitting procedure should report the selected measurements, objective, weights, parameter bounds, and uncertainty, with separate measurements reserved for evaluation.
Solvent-specific property, transport, kinetic, and enhancement correlations must then be supplied within their supported ranges.
Thermodynamic measurements excluded from fitting evaluate the parameterization, while independent absorber measurements evaluate the coupled column model.
An ePC-SAFT parameter file alone does not establish a second-solvent model.
