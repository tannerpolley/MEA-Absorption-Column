    \section{Results}
        The results are organized around one question at a time. The NCCC one-bed cases compare predicted capture and temperature profiles with observations from two no-intercooler pilot-plant campaigns. The thermodynamic comparison then changes only the CO$_2$ fugacity driving force. The solver comparison isolates numerical behavior, and the final timing test records a separate activity-rebased speciation calculation under feasibility settings.

    \subsection{ePC-SAFT Driving-Force Comparison}
        The Henry-law baseline uses a liquid Henry-law CO$_2$ fugacity and an ideal vapor partial-pressure expression. The ePC-SAFT calculation replaces that closure with vapor- and liquid-side fugacity coefficients computed from pressure-specified phase states. The liquid phase uses the ePC-SAFT electrolyte configuration described in Section~\ref{sec:model-framework}. The vapor-side calculation uses the same fugacity-coefficient framework with only neutral species present.

        \begin{center}
            \centering
            \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/nccc-one-bed-thermo-benchmark.pdf}
            \captionof{figure}{Thermodynamic driving-force comparison for one-bed, no-intercooler NCCC cases from the 2014 K and 2017 C campaigns that satisfied solver success, boundary-residual norm $\leq 1.0$, predicted capture between 0 and 100\%, and runtime $\leq\SI{90}{s}$. The left panel reports case-wise capture error, and the right panel reports median runtime. K20 is excluded because neither thermodynamic row satisfied all four criteria.}
            \label{fig:nccc-one-bed-thermo-benchmark}
        \end{center}

        Figure~\ref{fig:nccc-one-bed-thermo-benchmark} is the main thermodynamic comparison. Across the included one-bed rows, ePC-SAFT gives a capture mean absolute error of 3.32 percentage points, compared with 3.14 percentage points for Henry-law closure. Its median runtime is \SI{7.70}{s}, compared with \SI{5.25}{s} for Henry-law closure. The 0.18-percentage-point difference in mean absolute error does not show lower aggregate capture error for ePC-SAFT; the calculation instead quantifies the effect and runtime of replacing the Henry-law driving force with vapor- and liquid-side fugacity coefficients.

    \subsection{Numerical Solver Behavior}
        The solver comparison answers a different question than the thermodynamic comparison. It tests how shooting, collocation, and finite difference behave when they are applied to the same absorber equations under representative operating conditions. A smooth one-bed operating point is favorable to all three methods. The NCCC 3C case is more demanding because the measured profile contains a sharper temperature feature.

        Table~\ref{tab:method-case-contrast} and Figure~\ref{fig:method-case-solver-contrast} show why collocation is used for the routine NCCC rows. Shooting required \SI{4.91}{s} for the smooth comparison point but did not return a solution satisfying the reported criteria for NCCC 3C. In a separate high-liquid-to-gas-ratio probe, shooting returned 20 of 25 explicit-vapor-water rows with a median model runtime of \SI{0.78}{s} and 25 of 25 rows under the older vapor-water-ratio assumption with a median model runtime of \SI{0.85}{s}. These counts and runtimes describe numerical behavior only. The smooth-case finite-difference calculation returned a boundary-residual norm of $9.93\times10^{-13}$ but predicted \SI{107.65}{\percent} capture, demonstrating that a low boundary residual does not establish physical validity. For NCCC 3C, collocation returned \SI{89.40}{\percent} capture in \SI{9.40}{s}. Final mesh size, refinement history, and method-specific evaluation counts were not retained.

    \subsection{Activity-Rebased Ionic Speciation Timing Test}
        The routine one-bed campaign keeps the concentration-based chemical-equilibrium calculation fixed and uses ePC-SAFT for the CO$_2$ fugacity coefficients. A separate ionic activity-speciation calculation tested numerical coupling between ePC-SAFT activity-related quantities and chemical equilibrium. The calculation used a nine-species ionic state for CO$_2$, MEA, H$_2$O, MEAH$^+$, MEACOO$^-$, HCO$_3^-$, $\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}$, $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$, and $\mathrm{OH^-}$. For each local chemistry call, it evaluated the ePC-SAFT activity quotient at the concentration-based initial composition and used that quotient as the local reaction constant. It did not use an independent set of standard-state activity-basis $K_r(T)$ values. The solver returned all seven 2017 C-case rows with no invalid states, no domain guards, and chemistry residuals below $10^{-8}$ under the feasibility settings reported below.

        \input{tables/full_ionic_speciation_timing}

Table~\ref{tab:full-ionic-speciation-timing} compares this calculation with the routine rows. The activity-rebased calculation uses feasibility settings of 7 starting mesh points, a solver tolerance of 10, a boundary-condition tolerance of 0.5, and a maximum of 80 mesh nodes. The nine-species Case 3C row predicts $\SI{88.736}{\percent}$ capture, $-0.764$ percentage points from the measured value, and requires \SI{139.420}{s}; \SI{102.267}{s} is spent in the ePC-SAFT chemistry solves. Across all seven C rows, the mean runtime is \SI{171.102}{s}, the mean chemistry-solve time is \SI{127.764}{s}, and the capture mean absolute error is 6.595 percentage points. These values establish only that the numerical activity calculation returned solutions under the stated feasibility settings. They do not establish predictive reactive ePC-SAFT chemistry because the reaction constants were not obtained independently of the initial state.

    \subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
        The absorber model is a simplified one-bed benchmark built from literature submodels for reaction enhancement, transport properties, hydraulics, and heat transfer. The primary physical-comparison set contains one-bed NCCC cases from the 2014 K and 2017 C campaigns. Attempted two-bed 2017 D cases and higher-height cases did not satisfy the stated solver, boundary-residual, physical-capture, and runtime criteria, so they are excluded from the reported aggregate. The thermodynamic conclusions remain conditional on the selected ePC-SAFT parameters and fixed transport correlations; this work includes neither parameter uncertainty propagation nor transport-correlation uncertainty propagation.

        The NCCC cases span several flows, loadings, compositions, and temperatures, but those inputs co-vary as validation observations. They do not form a controlled operating sweep. The model also omits a plant-level energy or economic objective and fixes the one-bed pressure gradient at zero. No optimal operating condition is inferred from these rows. Plant optimization would require predeclared decision variables, hydraulic and process constraints, an energy or cost objective, and uncertainty analysis.

        The governing balances can be applied to another solvent only after replacing the solvent-specific submodels. The present calculation uses an MEA species set, reaction network, equilibrium constants, physical-property and transport correlations, kinetics, and enhancement model. Extension to another amine requires a declared activity standard state and reaction network; pure, binary, association, and electrolyte parameters with documented sources and estimation or transfer procedures; solvent-specific property, transport, and kinetic correlations; and independent thermodynamic and absorber validation. An ePC-SAFT parameter file alone does not establish a second-solvent model.

        The routine ePC-SAFT campaign changes the CO$_2$ fugacity driving force while holding the concentration-based chemistry, enhancement factor, transport correlations, and governing balances fixed. Future reactive work should replace the local rebasing procedure with independently sourced or regressed activity-basis $K_r(T)$ values before using the nine-species path for prediction. Direct eNRTL comparisons should likewise be added only when the calibration choices and timing data are reported on the same basis as the present benchmark.
