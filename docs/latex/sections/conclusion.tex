    \section{Conclusion}
        This work uses a simplified MEA absorber model to compare numerical solution methods and thermodynamic driving-force assumptions under a fixed set of governing equations. The evidence extends beyond a single illustrative case by adding one-bed NCCC capture metrics, attempted-case accounting, solver information, boundary residuals, temperature-profile comparisons, method contrasts, and ePC-SAFT fugacity-coefficient calculations. The ePC-SAFT contribution is an absorber-column comparison in which a liquid-side ionic ePC-SAFT CO$_2$ fugacity calculation is evaluated inside the thermodynamic driving force while concentration-based chemical equilibrium, enhancement, transport, hydraulics, and balances remain fixed. The vapor-side fugacity is evaluated with the same formulation in its neutral limit.

        Both thermodynamic calculations satisfied the solver-success, boundary-residual, physical-capture, and \qty{90}{s} criteria for nine one-bed NCCC cases. ePC-SAFT gave a capture mean absolute error of 3.32 percentage points, compared with 3.14 percentage points for Henry-law closure, while median runtime increased from \qty{5.25}{s} to \qty{7.70}{s}. The nine-species activity-rebased calculation returned all seven 2017 C rows with chemistry residuals below $10^{-8}$, zero invalid states, and zero guard penalties; its capture mean absolute error was 6.595 percentage points, its maximum absolute error was 16.514 percentage points, and its mean runtime was \qty{171.102}{s}. Because each local reaction constant was anchored to the concentration-based initial composition, this calculation demonstrates numerical activity coupling rather than independently predictive reactive thermodynamics. A separate typed-bundle diagnostic verified homogeneous equilibrium residuals, tangent symmetry, positive constrained curvature, and the zero-current/zero-flow limiting structure of an exploratory Onsager closure, while also showing a reaction-to-diffusion time-scale ratio of about $7.6\times10^{3}$. Those checks establish internal consistency and stiffness, not source-complete mobility, predictive reactive chemistry, or column transfer. The routine results quantify the capture error and runtime obtained when an electrolyte equation-of-state fugacity driving force replaces the Henry-law closure. The solver comparison separately quantifies method-dependent runtime, boundary residual, and unsuccessful calculations under the same governing equations.

        These conclusions apply to the selected MEA parameter set, fixed transport and enhancement correlations, one-bed NCCC case set, and retained numerical diagnostics. The study does not propagate thermodynamic or transport uncertainty, establish independent cross-facility validation, identify an optimal operating condition, or demonstrate extension to another amine. Predictive nine-species chemistry requires independently sourced activity-basis equilibrium constants and a parameter set with documented source and estimation records. Extension to another solvent requires solvent-specific chemistry, thermodynamic parameters, properties, kinetics, and absorber validation.
