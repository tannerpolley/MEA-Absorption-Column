    \section{Conclusion}
        Across seven NCCC one-bed cases, the coupled nine-species model gives a capture mean absolute error of \num{5.85} percentage points; Cases 6C and 7C retain substantial errors of \num{+11.45} and \num{-11.93} points. The refined Case 3C calculation predicts \qty{91.55}{\percent} capture for Case 3C against \qty{89.50}{\percent} measured, with a peak liquid temperature of \qty{347.86}{K}. Joint mesh/tolerance refinement changes capture by \num{0.00510} percentage points and peak temperature by \qty{0.04493}{K}, providing a numerical reference for the local sensitivity studies.

        In the historical six-species comparison of nine one-bed NCCC cases, the ePC-SAFT fugacity closure gave a capture mean absolute error of \num{3.32} percentage points, compared with \num{3.14} percentage points for Henry-law closure. Median runtime increased from \qty{5.25}{s} to \qty{7.70}{s}. Thus, replacing the fugacity driving force under fixed chemical-equilibrium, enhancement, and transport formulations increased computational cost without reducing aggregate capture error for this case set.

        Among the historical method-comparison configurations, shooting had the shorter recorded runtime at the smooth operating point, while collocation returned the accepted NCCC 3C solution. Residuals and physical admissibility must be assessed together when interpreting method performance.

        For the coupled nine-species Case 3C reference, five-percent interaction and equilibrium-constant perturbations change capture by up to \num{0.82} percentage points. MEA--water gives the largest response among the four tested thermodynamic inputs, while the two reaction constants change capture by approximately \num{0.42} and \num{0.60} points. Ten-percent transport perturbations change capture by up to \num{0.53} points, with the liquid-side transfer coefficient giving the largest response among the three transport inputs. Their peak-temperature changes remain below the reference refinement difference. These findings apply to the tested parameters and one-bed conditions. Joint parameter/correlation uncertainty and controlled variation of liquid-to-gas ratio, lean loading, and inlet temperature remain necessary to assess their generality. Extension to other solvents or multicomponent industrial flue gases requires corresponding chemistry and property descriptions; finite-rate liquid-film reaction and diffusion require an independently evaluated reaction--transport model.
