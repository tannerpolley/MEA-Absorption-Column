    \section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
        Aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) absorption is a common reference process for post-combustion \COtwo{} capture. Packed-column predictions depend on both the physical model and the numerical solution method, so comparisons require common equations, operating inputs, and evaluation criteria.

        This work evaluates a steady-state MEA absorber with coupled nine-species reactive ePC-SAFT, conventional enhancement-factor transport and nonisothermal column balances. Seven NCCC cases compare predicted capture and phase temperatures with observations; Case 3C additionally tests numerical resolution and local thermodynamic and transport sensitivities. A historical nine-case NCCC comparison is retained as a separate experiment: it replaces the Henry-law \COtwo{} driving force with EOS fugacity while keeping a reduced six-species chemical-equilibrium formulation fixed. Shooting, finite-difference and collocation results are distinguished by their physical and numerical configurations.

        \subsection{Literature Review}

        Rate-based MEA absorber modeling has an established literature. Foundational and subsequent studies have coupled chemical reaction, vapor-liquid equilibrium, transport, hydraulics, and energy balances to predict pilot-scale \COtwo{} absorption behavior \cite{Freguia2003,Mores2012,Zhang2009}. Related process-simulation studies have extended this approach toward load variation, solvent comparison, process control, and design analysis \cite{Moioli2012,Harun2012a,Greer2008,Rodriguez2014}. These studies establish the relevance of mechanistic absorber models and the need to report inputs, physical comparisons, and numerical diagnostics.

        Prior MEA absorber studies have used pilot-scale or campaign data to constrain and evaluate rate-based models, including packed-column validation, hydraulic and mass-transfer modeling, and uncertainty-oriented procedures \cite{Razi2013,Chinen2018,Morgan2018,Morgan2020}. This benchmark reports case-wise capture error, temperature-profile error where measured taps are available, boundary residuals, solver messages, and runtime across the evaluated one-bed NCCC cases.

        \Cref{tab:absorber-literature-comparison} shows that resolved-film calculations, enhancement approximations, experimental comparisons and numerical-resolution checks already exist in the absorber literature. Their thermodynamic descriptions, calibration data, numerical settings and reported inputs differ. The questions addressed here are how a coupled reactive EOS affects the solved absorber state and its input sensitivities, and how selected closures and numerical methods compare within explicitly matched model configurations. This complements, rather than replaces, the broader physical validation in those studies.

        Thermodynamic model choice is another source of ambiguity. Electrolyte activity-coefficient models such as electrolyte nonrandom two-liquid (eNRTL) represent liquid-phase excess Gibbs energy and species activities through fitted interaction parameters and a declared calibration procedure \cite{Zhang2011}. SAFT-family equations of state represent associating systems \cite{Gross2001,Cameretti2005}, and ePC-SAFT includes electrolyte contributions. SAFT-family models have also been applied to \COtwo{} solubility in aqueous MEA with explicit association-scheme choices \cite{Baygi2015,Najafloo2018}, while recent acid-gas absorption studies apply ePC-SAFT to reactive aqueous amine systems \cite{Cleeton2020,Bulow2021a}. This literature motivates the present comparison of Henry-law and electrolyte-EOS fugacity driving forces. In the historical six-species comparison, concentration-based equilibrium is fixed while the fugacity closure changes. The coupled nine-species calculation instead uses EOS activities in the reaction equations and a consistent bulk-fugacity/free-concentration film coefficient.

        Component-based ePC-SAFT inputs and combining rules can reduce additional mixture-specific fitting when applicable component and cross-interaction information is available. CPA also reuses component parameters and association physics, while eNRTL permits parameter reuse and fixed defaults. For example, Akula et al.\ \cite{Akula2023a} fit ten scalars: four interaction, four formation-property and two heat-capacity quantities (Table 6), in addition to inherited inputs including Hessen W2 neutral-mixture parameters. Zhang et al.\ \cite{Zhang2011} report four MEA--water interaction scalars (Table 8) and twelve ternary-fit quantities: six interactions, four formation properties and two heat capacities (Table 10). The selected ePC-SAFT set uses one neutral-binary refit, seven inherited ion-fit quantities (six component values and one pair coefficient), a grid-selected \COtwo{} dispersion value and two fitted reaction-temperature directions. Its remaining inputs are inherited, fixed or derived; these stage-specific counts do not establish universally fewer parameters or easier regression. Analytic or automatically differentiated Jacobians and Hessians are also not exclusive to ePC-SAFT. Accuracy and transferability require matched calibration and held-out comparisons.

        Numerical method comparisons in absorber models must also be interpreted carefully. Shooting, finite-difference, and collocation BVP methods are established numerical methods, but their results depend on scaling, initialization, mesh size, residual definitions, and physical-domain constraints. Applying the same model, data, and inclusion criteria permits comparison of recorded runtime, residuals, and unsuccessful calculations without treating a solver success message as physical validation.

    \subsection{Scope and Contributions}
        The literature contains many MEA absorber models, but differences in thermodynamic assumptions, solver formulations, inclusion criteria, and physical-comparison subsets can obscure whether a discrepancy arises from the numerical method or model form. The comparisons make four contributions.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item It evaluates a coupled nine-species, five-reaction ePC-SAFT model within a nonisothermal MEA absorber using conventional enhancement-factor transport.
            \item It reports capture and temperature profiles for seven NCCC cases, with Case 3C fugacity and speciation profiles, mesh/tolerance refinement and computational cost.
            \item It quantifies one-at-a-time sensitivity to selected binary interactions, reaction equilibrium constants, viscosity, diffusivity and liquid-side mass transfer.
            \item It retains the historical nine-case, six-species fugacity comparison and method-specific outcomes as explicitly separate calculations, including unsuccessful attempts.
        \end{itemize}
        The distinct formulations and parameters in each comparison are identified in \cref{tab:thermodynamic-responsibility}; results from different configurations are not pooled as repeated calculations.
