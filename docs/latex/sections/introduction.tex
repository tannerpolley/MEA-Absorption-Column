    \section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
        Aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) absorption is a common reference process for post-combustion CO$_2$ capture, and packed-column models of this process have an extensive published basis. A new comparison must therefore identify the changed assumptions, the observations used for evaluation, and the remaining model limitations rather than rely on a single pilot-plant case.

        The present work uses a steady-state MEA absorber model to compare numerical solution methods and CO$_2$ fugacity driving-force assumptions under one set of governing equations. The physical submodels form a simplified rate-based model assembled from literature correlations. The paper does not claim that those correlations, the electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) equations of state, or the boundary-value algorithms are new. The thermodynamic contribution is a packed-column comparison that isolates a liquid-side ionic ePC-SAFT CO$_2$ fugacity closure while holding chemistry, enhancement, transport, hydraulics, balances, solver settings, and row-inclusion criteria fixed. The liquid-side calculation uses ePC-SAFT for the MEA--CO$_2$--water electrolyte state, while the vapor-side calculation applies the same fugacity formulation to neutral species. The equations, National Carbon Capture Center (NCCC) case inputs, initialization rules, solver settings, thermodynamic options, inclusion criteria, and reported quantities are held fixed across the comparison.

Two needs motivate this benchmark. Solver behavior should be reported with enough numerical detail to recalculate capture and temperature predictions from the stated inputs and settings. The effect of replacing a Henry-law CO$_2$ driving force with an equation-of-state fugacity calculation should also be isolated before reaction chemistry, transport correlations, or other model assumptions change. The benchmark evaluates shooting, finite-difference, and collocation BVP solution methods, and it compares a Henry-law baseline with an ePC-SAFT fugacity-coefficient calculation that changes only the CO$_2$ fugacity driving force. In this paper, an ``ePC-SAFT fugacity benchmark'' means this controlled replacement of the local phase-state-to-fugacity calculation under fixed chemistry, transport, hydraulics, balances, solver settings, and row-inclusion criteria. It does not mean that ePC-SAFT supplies the reaction network or the equilibrium constants in the routine campaign.

        \subsection{Literature Review}

        Rate-based MEA absorber modeling has an established literature. Foundational and subsequent studies have coupled chemical reaction, vapor-liquid equilibrium, transport, hydraulics, and energy balances to predict pilot-scale CO$_2$ absorption behavior \cite{Freguia2003,Mores2012,Zhang2009}. Related process-simulation studies have extended this approach toward load variation, solvent comparison, process control, and design analysis \cite{Moioli2012,Harun2012a,Greer2008,Rodriguez2014}. These studies establish the relevance of mechanistic absorber models and the need to report inputs, physical comparisons, and numerical diagnostics.

        Physical comparison is central to this manuscript. Prior MEA absorber studies have used pilot-scale or campaign data to constrain and evaluate rate-based models, including packed-column validation, hydraulic and mass-transfer modeling, and uncertainty-oriented procedures \cite{Razi2013,Chinen2018,Morgan2018,Morgan2020}. A single successful case shows only that the model executes for that operating point. This benchmark reports case-wise capture error, temperature-profile error where measured taps are available, boundary residuals, solver messages, and runtime across the evaluated one-bed NCCC cases.

        Thermodynamic model choice is another source of ambiguity. Electrolyte activity-coefficient models such as electrolyte nonrandom two-liquid (eNRTL) represent liquid-phase excess Gibbs energy and species activities through fitted interaction parameters and a declared calibration procedure \cite{Zhang2011}. SAFT-family equations of state provide a route for associating systems \cite{Gross2001,Cameretti2005}, and ePC-SAFT extends that route with electrolyte contributions. SAFT-family models have also been applied to CO$_2$ solubility in aqueous MEA with explicit association-scheme choices \cite{Baygi2015,Najafloo2018}, while recent acid-gas absorption studies apply ePC-SAFT to reactive aqueous amine systems \cite{Cleeton2020,Bulow2021a}. That literature motivates an equation-of-state (EOS) driving-force comparison, but it does not by itself validate the present column model. In this work, ePC-SAFT is not introduced as a new thermodynamic theory or as a complete MEA thermodynamic replacement. It computes residual-Helmholtz, fugacity-coefficient, and activity-related thermodynamic quantities for the CO$_2$ driving force. This isolates the effect of replacing the Henry-law fugacity closure without simultaneously changing reaction chemistry, enhancement factors, or transport correlations. The routine ePC-SAFT calculation is therefore a controlled electrolyte-EOS CO$_2$ fugacity closure whose computational cost is reported for the evaluated column cases.

        Numerical method comparisons in absorber models must also be interpreted carefully. Shooting, finite-difference, and collocation BVP methods are established numerical methods, but their results depend on scaling, initialization, mesh size, residual definitions, and physical-domain constraints. This manuscript treats them as specified solver configurations rather than as new algorithms. Applying the same model, data, and inclusion criteria permits comparison of recorded runtime, residuals, and unsuccessful calculations without treating a solver success message as physical validation.

    \subsection{Scope and Contributions}
        The literature contains many MEA absorber models, but differences in thermodynamic assumptions, solver formulations, inclusion criteria, and physical-comparison subsets can obscure whether a discrepancy arises from the numerical method or model form. This work makes four bounded contributions.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item It provides a common benchmark in which shooting, finite-difference, and collocation BVP solution paths are evaluated against the same absorber equations and case data.
            \item It expands the physical comparison beyond the original Case 3C illustration to nine one-bed NCCC cases satisfying the stated solver, boundary-residual, capture-range, and runtime criteria, with K20 retained in attempted-case accounting.
            \item It embeds a liquid-side ionic ePC-SAFT CO$_2$ fugacity-coefficient calculation in a solved packed-column absorber comparison while keeping chemical equilibrium, enhancement factors, transport, hydraulics, and balances fixed.
            \item It records solver settings, solver messages, residual norms, runtime, and row-inclusion criteria so the numerical comparison can be checked from the reported records.
        \end{itemize}
        Each claim is bounded by a reported case set, solver configuration, thermodynamic option, and runtime.
