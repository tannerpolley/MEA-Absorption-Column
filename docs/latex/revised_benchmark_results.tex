    \subsection{Thermodynamic Driving-Force Comparison}
        The baseline thermodynamic lane uses the existing Henry-law calculation for the liquid CO$_2$ fugacity and an ideal vapor partial-pressure expression for the vapor fugacity. The ePC-SAFT lane uses the external ePC-SAFT package as a read-only dependency and computes the CO$_2$ fugacity coefficient from pressure-specified phase states. PC-SAFT and ePC-SAFT are established equation-of-state approaches for associating and electrolyte-containing systems \cite{Gross2001PC-SAFT:Molecules,Cameretti2005ModelingOriginal}. PC-SAFT and SAFT-family parameters for CO$_2$ solubility in aqueous MEA have been reported by Fakouri Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh and by Najafloo and Zarei \cite{FakouriBaygi2015ApplicationSolutions,Najafloo2018}, and ePC-SAFT acid-gas absorption studies provide additional context for EOS-based amine-solution calculations \cite{Cleeton2020CompetitiveEPC-SAFT,Bulow2021PredictingEPC-SAFT}.

        In this work, ePC-SAFT changes only the CO$_2$ fugacity driving force:
        \begin{align}
            f_{\COtwo}^{v} &= y_{\COtwo}\phi_{\COtwo}^{v}P,\\
            f_{\COtwo}^{\liq} &= x_{\COtwo}^{\liq}\phi_{\COtwo}^{\liq}P.
        \end{align}
        Chemical equilibrium, ionic speciation, enhancement factors, transport properties, heat-transfer correlations, and column balances are otherwise held fixed. This makes the ePC-SAFT comparison a controlled thermodynamic sensitivity study rather than a fully coupled electrolyte-reactive absorber model. The benchmark therefore evaluates whether replacing the Henry-law driving force changes capture accuracy, temperature error, convergence, and runtime under the same absorber equations.

    \subsection{Accuracy-Credibility Screens}
        The reviewer-facing concern is not only whether the model can converge, but whether the claims match the evidence. For this reason, the benchmark includes a no-case-specific-tuning gate, error-regime plots, a small global residual-correction screen, and uncertainty bands. These additions do not prove broad predictive accuracy. They make the evidence auditable and show where the one-bed model is more or less adequate.

        Figure~\ref{fig:error-regime-capture-error} plots capture error against three operating descriptors: liquid-to-gas ratio, lean loading, and inlet CO$_2$ mole fraction. The purpose is to identify error regimes instead of reporting only an aggregate mean error. Large errors remain visible for difficult cases, which keeps the validation claim conservative.

    \subsection{Model Adequacy}
        The model is considered adequate for a validation row only when convergence, boundary residuals, capture error, and temperature-profile error are all acceptable for the stated purpose. A row with low residuals but poor capture or temperature agreement is a useful diagnostic row, not proof of predictive accuracy. Under that standard, the present model is best interpreted as a transparent one-bed benchmark and thermodynamic-driving-force sensitivity platform. It provides a stronger basis for comparing solver behavior and thermodynamic assumptions than a single successful case study, while leaving broader predictive calibration as future work.
