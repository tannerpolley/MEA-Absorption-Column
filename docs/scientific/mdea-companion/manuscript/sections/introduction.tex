    \section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
        Aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) absorption remains a common reference process for post-combustion CO$_2$ capture because it combines mature solvent chemistry with well-studied packed-column equipment. The same maturity also raises the standard for new modeling papers. A useful contribution must do more than reproduce a single pilot-plant case with established correlations; it must clarify what is being benchmarked, which assumptions are changed, which data support the conclusions, and where the model remains incomplete.

        The present work addresses that narrower need. It uses a steady-state MEA absorber model to benchmark numerical solution paths and CO$_2$ fugacity driving-force assumptions under one set of governing equations. The physical submodels are treated as a simplified rate-based model assembled from literature correlations. The paper does not claim that those correlations, the electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) family of equations of state, or the boundary-value algorithms are new. To the authors' knowledge, the specific thermodynamic contribution is the first reproducible MEA packed-column benchmark to isolate a liquid-side ionic ePC-SAFT CO$_2$ fugacity closure under fixed chemistry, enhancement, transport, hydraulics, balances, solver settings, and validation gates. The liquid-side calculation uses ePC-SAFT for the MEA--CO$_2$--water electrolyte state, while the vapor-side calculation uses the same fugacity framework with only neutral species present. The broader contribution is the controlled comparison produced when the equations, National Carbon Capture Center (NCCC) case inputs, initialization rules, solver settings, thermodynamic model, acceptance criteria, and reporting structure are held fixed.

Two needs motivate this benchmark. Solver behavior should be reported with enough numerical detail that capture and temperature predictions can be reproduced rather than treated as isolated case studies. The effect of replacing a Henry-law CO$_2$ driving force with an equation-of-state fugacity calculation should also be isolated before reaction chemistry, transport correlations, or other model assumptions change. The benchmark evaluates shooting, finite-difference, and collocation BVP solution paths, and it compares a Henry-law baseline with an ePC-SAFT fugacity-coefficient calculation that changes only the CO$_2$ fugacity driving force. In this paper, an ``ePC-SAFT fugacity benchmark'' means this controlled replacement of the local phase-state-to-fugacity calculation under fixed chemistry, transport, hydraulics, balances, solver settings, and acceptance gates. It does not mean that ePC-SAFT supplies the reaction network or the equilibrium constants in the routine campaign.

        \subsection{Literature Review}

        Rate-based MEA absorber modeling is a mature area. Foundational and subsequent studies have coupled chemical reaction, vapor-liquid equilibrium, transport, hydraulics, and energy balances to predict pilot-scale CO$_2$ absorption behavior \cite{Freguia2003,Mores2012,Zhang2009}. Related process-simulation studies have extended this approach toward load variation, solvent comparison, process control, and design analysis \cite{Moioli2012,Harun2012a,Greer2008,Rodriguez2014}. These studies establish the relevance of mechanistic absorber models, but they also show that a new model must be evaluated with reproducible inputs, validation evidence, and reported numerical behavior rather than by novelty alone.

        Validation practice is central to this manuscript. Prior MEA absorber studies have used pilot-scale or campaign data to constrain and evaluate rate-based models, including packed-column validation, hydraulic and mass-transfer modeling, and uncertainty-oriented procedures \cite{Razi2013,Chinen2018,Morgan2018,Morgan2020}. A single successful case can show that a model executes, but it does not demonstrate reliability across the operating range. This benchmark reports case-wise capture error, temperature-profile error where measurements are available, boundary residuals, solver messages, and runtime across the available one-bed NCCC validation cases.

        Thermodynamic model choice is another source of ambiguity. Electrolyte activity-coefficient models such as electrolyte nonrandom two-liquid (eNRTL) can represent MEA chemistry with high fidelity but require many fitted interaction parameters and careful calibration \cite{Zhang2011}. SAFT-family equations of state provide a route for associating systems \cite{Gross2001,Cameretti2005}, and ePC-SAFT extends that route with electrolyte contributions. SAFT-family models have also been applied to CO$_2$ solubility in aqueous MEA with explicit association-scheme choices \cite{Baygi2015,Najafloo2018}, while recent acid-gas absorption studies demonstrate the continuing use of ePC-SAFT frameworks for reactive aqueous amine systems \cite{Cleeton2020,Bulow2021a}. That literature motivates an equation-of-state (EOS) driving-force comparison, but it does not by itself validate the present column model. In this work, ePC-SAFT is not introduced as a new thermodynamic theory or as a complete MEA thermodynamic replacement. It is used to compute residual-Helmholtz, fugacity-coefficient, and activity-related thermodynamic quantities for the CO$_2$ driving force. That isolates the effect of replacing the Henry-law fugacity closure without simultaneously changing reaction chemistry, enhancement factors, or transport correlations. This makes the routine ePC-SAFT calculation a controlled electrolyte-EOS CO$_2$ fugacity closure: more structured than an ideal or Henry-law driving-force closure, but still light enough for repeated column simulations when only the fugacity driving force is being tested.

        Numerical method comparisons in absorber models must also be interpreted carefully. Shooting, finite-difference, and collocation BVP methods are standard tools, but their behavior depends on scaling, initialization, mesh size, residual definitions, and physical-domain constraints. This manuscript treats the numerical methods as benchmarked solver configurations rather than as new algorithms. The value of the comparison is that the same model, data, and acceptance criteria expose where each method is fast, where it is fragile, and where solver convergence should not be confused with physical validation.

    \subsection{Scope and Contributions}
        The literature contains many capable MEA absorber models. Current comparison practice still limits reproducibility because studies use different thermodynamic assumptions, solver formulations, acceptance criteria, and validation subsets. Those differences make solver behavior difficult to distinguish from model-form error. This work makes four bounded contributions.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item It provides a common benchmark in which shooting, finite-difference, and collocation BVP solution paths are evaluated against the same absorber equations and case data.
            \item It expands validation beyond the original Case 3C illustration to nine solver-accepted one-bed NCCC cases, with K20 retained in attempted-case accounting.
            \item It isolates, to the authors' knowledge, the first liquid-side ionic ePC-SAFT CO$_2$ fugacity-coefficient calculation embedded in a solved packed-column absorber benchmark, while keeping chemical equilibrium, enhancement factors, transport, hydraulics, and balances fixed.
            \item It records solver settings, convergence messages, residual norms, runtime, and benchmark gates so the numerical comparison can be checked and reproduced.
        \end{itemize}
        These contributions are intended to make the benchmark useful for comparison and follow-on development: each claim is tied to a reported case set, solver configuration, thermodynamic option, and runtime.
